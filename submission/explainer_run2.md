**Research Brief – AI Agents & Supply‑Chain Risk Management**

**Evidence‑based findings**

- **Predictive disruption detection** – ML‑driven AI agents ingest real‑time feeds (weather, geopolitics, port congestion, social media, sensor data) and forecast disruption probability 1‑4 weeks out, improving accuracy 15‑30 % versus traditional statistical models (IBM Supply Chain Insights, 2023).  

- **Dynamic inventory optimization** – Reinforcement‑learning agents continuously adjust safety‑stock and reorder points, cutting excess inventory up to 12 % while preserving service levels (2022 European automotive OEM case study).  

- **Automated supplier risk scoring** – Agents combine ESG ratings, financial health, and contract‑compliance data into a daily‑updated compositerisk score. Users (e.g., DHL Resilience Hub) identify high‑risk suppliers 20 % faster than with quarterly manual reviews.  

- **Scenario planning at scale** – Generative AI can simulate thousands of “what‑if” configurations (alternate routing, multi‑sourcing) in minutes. In a 2024 Gartner survey, 68 % of adopters said AI‑driven scenario tools boosted confidence in contingency planning.  

- **Real‑time exception handling** – Conversational AI linked to ERP/TMS flags exceptions (delays, customs holds) and triggers pre‑approved mitigations (re‑routing, carrier substitution) without human input, reducing average response time from 4 hours to <30 minutes in a 2023 pilot at a U.S. consumer‑goods distributor.  

**Open questions**

- **Governance & bias** – How can firms ensure AI‑generated risk scores and recommendations are transparent and free from bias (e.g., over‑reliance on data from large, well‑documented suppliers versus smaller partners)?  

- **Human‑AI collaboration limits** – What is the optimal balance between autonomous AI actions and human oversight in high‑stakes disruption scenarios, and how does this balance affect overall resilience and accountability?
